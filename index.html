M.S. Student in Statistics, Korea University

I am an M.S. student in Statistics at Korea University, where I work with Professor Sangbum Choi. I previously earned a B.A. in Public Health and a B.Econ. in Statistics from Korea University.

My research interests lie in survival analysis, competing risks, and causal inference for time-to-event data, with applications to real-world health data. My current research focuses on developing a partial likelihood approach for discrete-time competing risks using a Poisson approximation and trend filtering for baseline hazard estimation, with an application to cancer registry data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program. Building on this work, I aim to develop statistical methods for causal inference with survival and competing risks data to better understand treatment effects in observational studies.

A Partial-likelihood Approach for the Analysis of Discrete-Time Competing Risks Data
Seohyun Lee, Minjung Lee, and Sangbum Choi
  • Proposed a Poisson-approximation-based partial likelihood approach for discrete-time competing risks, including a Breslow-type baseline hazard estimator and cluster-robust sandwich variance estimation
  • Incorporated trend filtering for baseline hazards and implemented ADMM for penalized estimation
  • Evaluated finite-sample performance through simulations and applied the method to SEER data on 9,104 patients with stage III colon cancer

Selected Projects

Discrete-Time Survival Prediction with Machine Learning: Tutorial and Simulation Study Nonparametric Statistical Methods

Presented a tutorial on discrete-time survival modeling with machine learning based on Suresh et al. (2022), and compared Cox, random survival forest, discrete-time GLM, and neural network models in a Weibull simulation study in R.

Causal Effect of Social Interaction on Late-Life Depression (KLoSA) Korean Longitudinal Study of Aging

Estimated the causal effect of social interaction on depression in older adults using propensity score matching with bootstrap resampling, and identified nonlinear associations and high-risk subgroups with generalized additive models and decision trees.

Causal Debiasing for Recommendation Systems KuaiRec dataset · KUBIG Machine Learning Contest

Applied IPS-weighted BPR to reduce popularity bias in recommendations and Double Machine Learning to test whether popularity causally affects watch ratio on the KuaiRec dataset.

Predictive Model for Waterborne Infectious Disease Outbreaks KDCA infectious disease data · KUBIG Conference

Developed weekly forecasting models for bacterial, viral, and protozoan enteric infections from climate and flood data, comparing Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM with negative binomial regression for interpretability.

Nationwide Influenza-Like Illness (ILI) Activity Prediction U.S. influenza surveillance data · Time Series Analysis

Built a time series model of weekly national ILI activity, identifying seasonality and shifts in epidemic patterns during the COVID-19 period.
